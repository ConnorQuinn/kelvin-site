Very often we are engaged by a client to address a specific technical problem, but as soon as we arrive a different problem becomes immediately clear. 

**Each of the people in the data team is working frantically hard, but delivery takes ages and what eventually gets delivered doesn't impact the user.**

In our experience, this problem is rarely the result of a lack of talent, or a lack of work ethic. You have an alignment problem. 

There are two sides to tackling this problem.

The first part of the solution is down to leadership and direction. How can we get a cross-functional data team tightly aligned around delivering a clear solution?

The second part is more practical in nature. As a data initiative moves through its lifecycle, many different roles will be involved: BAs, Data Engineers, Data Architects, Data Scientists, BI Developers. Each of these roles speaks a different language and often the work they produce does not enable their peers to delivery effectively. So to resolve this challenge, we need to clearly articulate a series of workshops and artefacts that allow a lean, impactful analytical data product to be delivered fast.

This post discusses solutions to both sides of this problem.

### What is data value mapping?

Data value mapping is a framework that explicitly connects data initiatives to strategic business objectives. Rather than starting with available data and asking "what can we build?", it starts with business decisions and asks "what data would improve this outcome?"

The approach involves three core activities:

**1. Decision mapping**: Identify the key decisions that drive business performance. For each decision, document who makes it, how often, what information they currently use, and what outcome they're trying to optimise. This creates a clear line of sight from data to action.

**2. Value articulation**: For each decision, quantify the potential value of better data. This might be revenue uplift from improved targeting, cost reduction from optimised operations, or risk mitigation from earlier detection. Without clear value articulation, every request appears equally important and prioritisation becomes impossible.

**3. Initiative alignment**: Map existing and proposed data initiatives against the decision framework. This exposes work that doesn't connect to business outcomes and highlights gaps where high-value decisions lack adequate data support.

### From silos to delivery

Data value mapping alone won't fix a fragmented team. When business analysts, BI developers, data engineers, and data scientists work in isolation, they each optimise for their own outputs rather than collective business impact. Breaking this pattern requires structured intervention.

At Kelvin Analytics, we run a series of targeted workshops that bring cross-functional team members together around specific, high-value use cases. Rather than abstract discussions about collaboration, we focus the team on delivering a tangible piece of impactful analytics within a short timeframe.

This approach achieves two things simultaneously. First, it delivers incremental value to the business, demonstrating that the data function can move quickly when properly aligned. Second, it rebuilds confidence with stakeholders who may have grown sceptical after months of promised deliverables that never materialised. Each successful delivery creates momentum for the next, establishing a pattern of execution that the team can sustain independently.

### A framework for prioritisation

Effective data value mapping uses a structured approach to prioritisation that considers both business impact and delivery feasibility. For each potential initiative, evaluate:

- **Strategic alignment**: Does this support the organisation's top priorities?
- **Expected value**: What is the quantified benefit if we succeed?
- **Readiness to act**: Is the business prepared to change behaviour based on the results?
- **Technical feasibility**: Do we have the data, skills, and infrastructure to deliver?

Initiatives that score highly on strategic alignment and expected value but poorly on readiness should be flagged for stakeholder development work. There is no point building a sophisticated recommendation engine if the sales team won't use it.

This framework ensures data teams focus on work where the business is ready to realise value, building credibility through quick wins while laying groundwork for larger transformations.

### Restoring business confidence

For data teams that have lost stakeholder trust, data value mapping provides a path back to credibility. The structured workshops create genuine dialogue between data practitioners and business users. Business leaders feel heard when their priorities explicitly shape the data roadmap. Data teams gain clarity on what success looks like and can demonstrate progress against agreed metrics.

The key is starting small and delivering quickly. Identify two or three high-value decisions where better data could make an immediate difference. Deliver working solutions in weeks rather than months. Use these quick wins to build momentum and demonstrate that the data team can deliver business value, not just technical outputs.
